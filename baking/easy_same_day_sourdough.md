# Never Dense Sourdough Bread in One Day 🍞

**From the video** by The Daily Bake (Barb Green): *Never Dense Sourdough Bread in One Day (Beginner Friendly)*

- **Hands-on time:** 30–40 min
- **Bake time:** 40–45 min
- **Yield:** 1 loaf

A crackly crust, open-crumb sourdough loaf mixed, shaped, and baked in a single day — no overnight bulk ferment. Four simple stretch-and-folds and a hot Dutch oven do the heavy lifting.

## 🌙 Overnight Starter Build (12 hours before baking)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ripe starter | 30 g (2 Tbsp) |
| Room-temperature water | 60 g (¼ cup) |
| Unbleached bread flour | 60 g (½ cup) |

Mix and let sit ~12 hours (overnight) until bubbly, doubled, and passing the float test.

## 🍞 Bake Day Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ripe starter | 125 g (½ cup) |
| Room-temperature water | 365 g (1½ cups + 2 tsp) |
| Bread flour | 500 g (4 cups, spooned and leveled) |
| Fine sea salt | 12 g (2 tsp) |

## Tools

- Kitchen scale
- Bench scraper
- Bowl or Danish dough whisk
- Proofing basket (banneton) + rice flour for dusting
- Bread lame (or sharp knife) for scoring
- 5–6 qt Dutch oven
- Instant-read thermometer (optional)

## Method

### 1. Check your starter
Your starter should be ripe and active — bubbly, doubled, and it should float in a glass of water (the **float test**). If not, feed it and wait.

### 2. Mix the dough (autolyse)
Combine the 125 g ripe starter and 365 g water in a large bowl. Add the 500 g bread flour and mix until no dry flour remains. Let the dough rest so the flour fully hydrates.

### 3. Add the salt
Sprinkle the 12 g fine sea salt over the dough (a little water helps it dissolve). Squeeze and pinch it in thoroughly until evenly distributed.

### 4. Four sets of stretch & folds
Over the next ~1½ hours, do **4 rounds of stretch & folds** (one every 20–30 minutes):
- Grab one side of the dough, stretch it upward, and fold it over the center.
- Rotate the bowl a quarter turn and repeat on all four sides.
- With each round the dough will feel smoother, stronger, and less sticky.

### 5. Bulk fermentation
Let the dough rise at warm room temperature (ideally ~75–78°F / 24–26°C) until puffed, jiggly, and roughly 50–75% bigger — typically 3–4 hours on a same-day schedule. Watch the dough, not the clock.

### 6. Pre-shape
Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface and shape into a loose round. Let it rest on the bench (bench rest) to relax, about 20–30 minutes.

### 7. Final shape
Shape the dough into a tight boule: fold the edges into the center, flip seam-side down, and drag it in circles to build surface tension. Place seam-side up in a rice-floured banneton.

### 8. Cold proof & preheat
Cover the banneton and refrigerate for a short cold proof (about 1–2 hours) while you preheat the oven with the Dutch oven inside to 450–500°F (230–260°C).

### 9. Score the loaf
Turn the chilled dough out onto parchment, dust the top with flour, and score with a lame — one confident slash or a simple pattern, about ½ inch deep.

### 10. Dutch oven bake
Lower the loaf (with parchment) into the hot Dutch oven:
- **Lid on:** 20 minutes at ~500°F (260°C) or 450°F (230°C)
- **Lid off:** 20–25 minutes until deep golden brown
- Internal temperature should reach ~205–210°F (96–99°C)

### 11. Cool completely — don't slice hot bread!
Transfer to a wire rack and cool at least 1 hour (longer is better) before slicing. Cutting too early gives a gummy crumb — patience is the last ingredient.

## Tips for a Never-Dense Loaf

- **Warm dough = same-day dough.** Keep bulk fermentation around 75–78°F; a cold kitchen will slow everything down.
- **Watch the dough, not the clock.** Under-proofing is the #1 cause of dense, gummy loaves.
- **Build tension when shaping** — a loose shape means a flat loaf.
- **Use rice flour in the banneton** — it won't burn or stick like wheat flour.
- **Cool fully** before slicing to set the open crumb.
