# Atole de Nuez 🥜☕

**From the video** by Cooking Con Claudia: *This ATOLE DE NUEZ is all you need this season!*

A warm, creamy Mexican pecan drink — toasted pecans blended with milk, piloncillo, and cinnamon, lightly thickened. Perfect for cold weather, holidays, or Noche Buena.

- **Yield:** about 6 cups
- **Time:** ~25 minutes

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Pecan halves | 4 oz (1 cup) |
| Milk | 7 cups |
| Water | 1 cup |
| Piloncillo cone | 1 (about 3–4 oz; sub dark brown sugar) |
| Cornstarch | 5 tbsp |
| Ground cinnamon | ½ tsp |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Vanilla (vanilla blend) | 1 tsp |
| Love 💕 | 1 serving |

## Instructions

1. **Toast the pecans.** In a dry pan over medium heat, toast the pecan halves 2–3 minutes, stirring often, until fragrant and lightly darkened. Don't let them burn.
2. **Dissolve the piloncillo.** In a large pot, combine the water, cinnamon stick, and piloncillo. Warm over medium-low heat until the piloncillo fully melts.
3. **Blend the pecan mixture.** In a blender, combine the toasted pecans, cornstarch, ground cinnamon, vanilla, and about half the milk. Blend until completely smooth, 1–2 minutes.
4. **Cook the atole.** Add the blended pecan mixture and the remaining milk to the pot. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly (a whisk or molinillo works well), until it thickens and coats the back of a spoon — about 10 minutes. Don't let it boil hard.
5. **Serve hot** in mugs, dusted with ground cinnamon and topped with a few chopped pecans.

## Tips

- **Stir constantly** once the cornstarch goes in — it thickens fast and can scorch.
- Found it too sweet? Commenters suggest using only half a piloncillo cone; you can always add more.
- It thickens as it cools — loosen with warm milk when reheating.
- **Substitutions:** dark brown sugar for piloncillo; masa harina for part of the cornstarch for a more traditional masa flavor; almond or oat milk for dairy-free.
- Leftovers keep a few days refrigerated; reheat gently while whisking.

## Serving Suggestions

Excellent alongside pan dulce, conchas, buñuelos, roles de canela, tamales, or your favorite cookies for dunking — a classic pairing for posadas and the holiday season.
